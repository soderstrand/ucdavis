Research

For more than five decades, Professor Michael A. Soderstrand's research has focused on the theory, design, and implementation of analog and digital circuits and systems, with particular emphasis on active filter design, digital signal processing, residue number system arithmetic, adaptive filtering, and high-performance computer architectures. His research has consistently combined mathematical rigor with practical engineering, producing innovations that have influenced both academic research and industrial applications. Over the course of his career he has authored or coauthored more than 200 refereed journal and conference publications, written numerous textbooks and book chapters, edited two landmark IEEE Press books, and holds three U.S. patents.

Professor Soderstrand's research career began at Sandia National Laboratories, where he worked from 1968 through 1979 as a Member of the Technical Staff and Project Leader. During this period he participated in a wide range of advanced engineering projects, including computer-based automatic test equipment, wireless telemetry systems for missile testing, and renewable energy systems. His most significant responsibility was serving as Project Manager for the Master Control System of Solar One, the pioneering central receiver solar power plant in Barstow, California. Solar One became the first mirror-based solar thermal generating plant to demonstrate continuous electric power production exceeding ten megawatts, establishing an important milestone in the development of utility-scale solar energy.

Upon joining the faculty of the University of California, Davis in 1972, Professor Soderstrand established an internationally recognized research program in Circuits and Systems. His laboratory attracted undergraduate students, graduate students, visiting scholars, and research collaborators from throughout the United States, Europe, Asia, and Latin America. Unlike many university research laboratories that focused almost exclusively on graduate students, the UC Davis program deliberately integrated undergraduate researchers into active research teams, providing an exceptional pathway into research careers.

His research contributed to several emerging fields within electrical engineering. Early work in active RC filters led to the development of new low-sensitivity filter structures and eventually to the invention of Active-R Filters. Subsequent research pioneered the application of residue number system arithmetic to high-speed digital signal processing, enabling new architectures for digital filters and parallel computation. Later work expanded into adaptive digital filtering, pipelined digital filter architectures, system identification, VLSI implementations, wireless communications, FPGA-based signal processing, and high-performance digital hardware. The breadth of these contributions is reflected in a publication record spanning six decades and more than two hundred peer-reviewed technical papers.

Among Professor Soderstrand's most influential scholarly contributions were two IEEE Press books that helped define rapidly emerging areas of research. Modern Active Filter Design (1981) became a foundational reference in analog active filter design, while Residue Number System Arithmetic: Modern Applications in Digital Signal Processing (1986) helped establish residue number arithmetic as an important research area in digital signal processing and computer architecture. Both books appeared during the formative years of their respective fields and stimulated substantial subsequent research throughout the world.

Professor Soderstrand also provided extensive leadership to the international Circuits and Systems research community. He served as General Chair of five major international conferences: the 1997 IEEE Midwest Symposium on Circuits and Systems in Sacramento; the Thirty-Fifth Asilomar Conference on Signals, Systems and Computers in Pacific Grove in 2001; the 2002 IEEE Midwest Symposium on Circuits and Systems in Tulsa; the 2011 IEEE International Midwest Symposium on Circuits and Systems in Seoul, Republic of Korea; and the 2017 IEEE International Symposium on Circuits and Systems in Boston. In addition, he served as Vice President of the IEEE Circuits and Systems Society, Founding Editor of IEEE Circuits and Systems Magazine, Associate Editor of IEEE Transactions on Circuits and Systems, and Technical Program Chair for several additional international conferences.

The significance of Professor Soderstrand's research contributions was formally recognized in 1999 when he was elevated to the grade of IEEE Fellow, one of the highest honors bestowed by the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers. His elevation cited his contributions to analog and digital circuits and systems, recognizing both his pioneering technical achievements and his sustained leadership within the international Circuits and Systems community. He was subsequently named a Life Fellow of the IEEE, reflecting the enduring influence of his research, scholarship, and professional service.
